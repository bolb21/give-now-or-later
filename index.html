How this works

Giving now saves A × m_now ÷ C lives, where A is the amount, m is the tax multiplier and C is the cost of saving a life (£4,000, in line with GiveWell's top charities at $3,500–$5,500).

Investing for T years gives an expected:

A × (1+r)^T × (1−d)^T × m_later ÷ (C × (1+c)^T)

r is the real investment return, d is the yearly chance you stop giving, and c is how the cost of saving a life changes each year (negative if it gets cheaper). Everything is after inflation, so inflation cancels out. Waiting wins when the ratio of the two is above 1; within 10% either way, the calculator calls it close and suggests splitting.

Sources: UBS Global Investment Returns Yearbook (via Cambridge JBS), GiveWell top charities, value drift estimates (EA Forum), Gift Aid (GOV.UK), Phil Trammell on patient philanthropy.

This is a simple model for one donor. It leaves out knock-on benefits of helping people sooner, the chance that better opportunities are found later, and exchange rates.
